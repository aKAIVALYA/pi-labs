# STARLight x NVIDIA

Founder's Office media brief

Date: 18 September 2026
Request: Media interview

## Objective

Provide the founder's perspective on the STARLight-NVIDIA development, its market impact, whether it represents a genuine breakthrough, the hardware stakes, and what it signals for the industry.

## Key framing

STARLight is not an NVIDIA-STMicroelectronics joint venture. It is an EU CHIPS-funded consortium of 24 companies and universities across 11 countries, led by STMicroelectronics and running through 2028. NVIDIA is one contributing partner, not a co-founder.

## Research and business analysis

### Market impact

- NVDA fell 2.82% on the announcement date, 23 September 2025, and a further 0.82% the following day.
- The market response suggests a multi-year infrastructure story rather than an immediate NVIDIA catalyst.
- STMicroelectronics has more direct exposure through PIC100 high-volume production, announced in March 2026, with plans to quadruple capacity by 2027.
- At NVIDIA's approximately $5T scale, a photonics tailwind is unlikely to materially change the multiple. At STMicroelectronics' approximately $46B scale, it is structurally more significant.

### Breakthrough or industry trend?

STARLight is an industrialization milestone, not a new physics breakthrough. Silicon photonics has been identified as the next step in AI networking for years. STARLight adds the manufacturing layer: 300mm wafer production, packaging, and a sovereign European supply chain.

### Hardware significance

AI scaling is increasingly an interconnect problem as well as a compute problem. Co-packaged optics reduce electrical signal loss from roughly 22 dB to 4 dB and claim 3.5x better power efficiency than pluggable transceivers. GPUs are only as effective as the fabric connecting them, and that fabric is becoming harder to manufacture at scale.

## Talking points

1. **Interconnect speed is becoming the new AI moat.** The race is shifting from pure chip performance to infrastructure bottlenecks. A faster GPU means little if the fabric linking thousands of GPUs is constrained.
2. **Silicon photonics is reaching commercial maturity.** The change is manufacturing, not new physics: STARLight is building a 300mm, high-volume European supply chain for co-packaged optics.
3. **It solves a power problem as much as a speed problem.** Optical signaling offers a meaningful efficiency gain as data-center power demand strains the grid.
4. **NVIDIA's competitive footprint is widening.** The value proposition now spans GPUs, networking, and optics. NVIDIA's participation shows interdependence between US technology and European semiconductor manufacturing, not a bilateral deal.
5. **Commercialization is the number that matters.** ST moving PIC100 into high-volume production, with plans to quadruple capacity by 2027, is stronger evidence than a partnership announcement alone.

## Proposed founder quote

> "Silicon photonics isn't a new direction for our industry. That's been clear for years. What's changing is scale and economics. As AI clusters move toward millions of GPUs, the network connecting them becomes as critical as the processors themselves, and the power it draws becomes just as critical as its speed. STARLight matters because it's an EU-led effort, and NVIDIA is one of twenty-four partners in it, focused on turning co-packaged optics into high-volume manufacturing rather than a lab demo. The real story isn't a partnership. It's optical interconnects becoming a mainstream, industrialized part of AI infrastructure."
